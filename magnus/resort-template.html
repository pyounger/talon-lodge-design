Alaska’s Luxury Fishing Resort

The ultimate Alaska adventure resort and private island luxury fishing lodge — Sitka, Alaska

Fifteen minutes by boat from Sitka, Talon Lodge sits alone on a private island in the Tongass — the largest national forest in America, and the only temperate rain forest most guests will ever fish.

Four chefs. A three-thousand-bottle cellar. An open-air massage pavilion built out over the water. King salmon within thirty minutes of the dock. Twenty-four guests at a time.

It is a fishing lodge in the sense that a great restaurant is a place with chairs. The fishing is extraordinary — kings, silvers and Pacific halibut in the salt; steelhead, cutthroat and dolly varden in the freshwater of the Tongass. But guests tend to talk afterwards about the wood-fired soaking tub, or the night a visiting winemaker opened something from the vault that nobody expected to find in Southeast Alaska.

An island in the Inside Passage

Talon sits off Baranof Island, in the waters of Alaska’s Inside Passage, with the Tongass at its back and Mount Edgecumbe across the sound. The Bluff House — three bedrooms, its own view of the volcano — looks west over open Pacific.

Everything the lodge serves and every place it fishes is inside that frame. The prime saltwater grounds are half an hour out. The freshwater is reached by float plane, ATV or boat. Kruzof Island, with its ATV country and its volcano, is a morning’s run. You are not commuting to the wilderness; you are staying in it.

Four chefs, twenty-four guests

The ratio is the point. Three culinary masters run the kitchen through the season and a visiting chef series brings a fourth, cooking for a room of twenty-four. That is a restaurant ratio, not a lodge one.

The island supplies more of it than you would expect: wild berries from the property, an organic garden that has grown well past the word “kitchen,” and seafood landed the same day. Alaska-to-table is a phrase most places use loosely. Here the distance is measured in yards.

The cellar holds three thousand bottles and several hundred varietals. Its port and dessert selection has no equal in the Pacific Northwest — a strange sentence to write about a private island, and the reason winemakers keep coming back to pour at the guest winemaker events.

Late May to early September

Twenty-four guests at a time, so the season fills from the inside out. Check dates before you plan flights into Sitka.
