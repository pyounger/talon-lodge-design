System Blueprint · Guest Lifecycle

One guest, start to finish.

How the whole platform fits together — from the master record a guest is born into, through every room, boat, activity, meal and massage, to the survey score and notes that travel with them into the next season.

Draft for review For the Talon team & Stella dev v1 · September 2026 15 screens · working prototypes

This document walks the entire guest lifecycle in the order it actually happens, one stage at a time. Every stage shows the business rules that govern it, whether the screen is admin-facing or guest-facing, how it maps to the database, and a screenshot of the working prototype so there's no guessing what "done" looks like.

It has two audiences. For the Talon team, it's the place to confirm the rules match how the lodge really runs — mark anything that's wrong and we'll change it. For Stella's developers, it's the guide to what each screen does and the schema it writes to. Everything pictured is a real, clickable prototype; the closing section is honest about what's fully built versus what still needs the backend.

The one idea everything else hangs on

Everything funnels to the individual guest. The group is only how people book — every assignment, order, catch, score and note attaches to the one person, and accumulates across every visit, forever.

Phase A

Foundation

Before a single booking, two things must exist: one clean record per human being, and a catalog of everything the lodge can offer. The whole platform is built on these two.

01

The person record — the spine

People arrive through three different doors, but the system keeps exactly one master record per human. Every arrival is matched against who's already known; certain matches fold in, new people get a fresh record, and uncertain cases wait for a human. From then on, a person's whole history lives on that single record.

Admin · back office

    Business rules

  • One master Person per human — history only ever accumulates, it is never overwritten.
  • Three intake doors all run the same matching engine: the website contact form, a reservation booking, and the guest list.
  • Tiered matching: Tier 1 — exact email → auto-link · Tier 2 — phone + nickname-equivalent name (Bob = Robert) → auto-link · Tier 3 — name only, no contact → waits in a review queue for a human.
  • A role (lead / member) lives on a person's membership in a group, not on the person — so a past guest can become a lead later and the record shows both.
  • A booking agent or shared inbox is stored on the group, and is never merged into a guest's person record.
  • Nothing is ever hard-deleted (soft-delete only), and every merge is reversible — the losing and surviving records are snapshotted so a merge can be undone precisely.

Maps to schema

Intake rows (inquiries, leads, reservation_requests, guest_list_entries) each keep their raw data plus a person_id. The matcher resolves to one persons row with child person_emails · person_phones · person_name_aliases. Merges are logged in merge_history (undo-safe); unresolved Tier-3 cases sit in merge_review_queue.

02

The offerings — every asset

Rooms, activities, boats, guides, breakfast and beverages are all the same kind of thing: an asset with a type and a bag of properties. Setting them up once here is what lets every later screen assign them to guests. This is also the schema trick that lets the lodge add a new offering without a database migration.

Admin · setup

    Business rules

  • Every offering is an asset of a type — Room, Activity, Boat, Guide, Captain, Deckhand, Breakfast, Beverage — with a JSON properties bag (description, photos, capacity, price…).
  • Guides link to activities; captains and deckhands crew the boats — those relationships are stored as asset-to-asset links.
  • Breakfast is offered as 4–5 options per morning; beverages are sold by the 6-pack and stocked onto boats.
  • A package bundles assets (rooms + activities) as package assets, which is what drives availability and pricing later.
  • New field or new asset type? It's defined as data, so it appears everywhere without a code change.

Maps to schema

One assets table (asset_type + properties JSON) covers every offering; crew and guides attach via asset_links. Packages point at assets through package_assets. All type-specific fields are governed by field_definitions (the meta-schema) — no migration to add a room amenity or a new tour.

Phase B

Booking & pre-arrival

A booking turns a person into a booked guest, gathers their travelling party, and hands each of them a portal to get ready for the trip.

03

Booking & the guest list

The person who books becomes the group lead. After the reservation is confirmed, the system asks the lead for their guest list; each traveller is matched to the master database and gets their own portal invite. From that moment the lodge can see, per guest, exactly how ready they are for the trip.

Admin · rosterGuest · guest list

    Business rules

  • The booker becomes the group lead; a scheduled post-confirmation message requests the full guest list.
  • Each named traveller is matched to the master record (Stage 1) and linked to the group as a member, with their own portal invite.
  • Availability for a package = the rooms selected × their max occupancy; publishing is guarded so a package can't go live oversold.
  • A repeat-guest badge (R2, R3, R4…) surfaces returning guests at a glance, clickable through to their prior years.
  • Pre-trip readiness is tracked across seven dimensions — profile, address, sizing, emergency contact, health, airfare, fishing licence — so the team can chase exactly what's missing.

Maps to schema

A group is created with the lead on group_persons.role = lead; each guest becomes a member row. The booking is the trip (Group ↔ Trip is 1:1). Readiness is computed from each guest's trip_profiles completeness. Returning-guest counts come straight off the person's history across all trips.

04

The guest portal & profile

Between booking and arrival, the guest does their own preparation in a portal built for them — not the admin app. They complete a profile for this trip: personal details, dietary needs, emergency contact, flights, and their fishing licence. Anything they request is reviewed by the lodge within 24 hours.

Guest · self-serviceAdmin · reviews requests

    Business rules

  • The profile exists per trip and is copied forward from the guest's last visit — people change (boot size, diet, medical), so each trip snapshots its own, preserving history.
  • The guest captures dietary needs, allergies, emergency contact, flights in & out, and uploads their Alaska fishing licence.
  • Every activity or spa request the guest makes is reviewed within 24 hours before it's confirmed (Stage 6).
  • The portal is deliberately its own experience — no admin sidebar, luxury tone, real photography, no emoji.

Maps to schema

Writes a trip_profiles row (structured core fields + a details JSON governed by field_definitions), versioned and copied from the prior trip. The licence and photos become stay_media. Requests are written as pending assignments awaiting approval.

Phase C

Assignment

With the party known and the offerings set, the lodge places each individual guest — into a room, onto activities and the spa, and onto a boat. Every placement is per person, and every placement flows into the daily run sheet.

05

Rooms

The reservation assigns the room(s) to the group; here the team places each individual guest into one of them. Over-capacity is accepted and flagged, never blocked — the lodge always knows better than the software. Every placement attaches to the guest, so it flows straight to the rooming list and the daily report.

Admin · lodging

    Business rules

  • Each individual guest is placed into a specific room — not just the group into a building.
  • Over-capacity is accepted and flagged, never blocked.
  • Staff can move anyone to any room lodge-wide from one board (24 guests / 24 beds when the lodge is full).
  • The assignment attaches to the guest, so it appears on the rooming list and the daily run sheet automatically.

Maps to schema

A per-person assignment of a Room asset to the guest for the trip. The room-level occupancy view is derived; the source of truth is one row per guest.

06

Activities & spa

Every activity and spa request — whether it came from the reservation or from the guest's own portal — lands in one approval queue on a 24-hour clock. Approving confirms it and the guest sees it as scheduled. Massage is booked per person, with a therapist, treatment and time for each appointment.

Admin · concierge

    Business rules

  • Requests from both the reservation flow and the guest portal land in a single queue, each on a 24-hour review clock.
  • Approving sets the request to confirmed; the guest immediately sees it as "scheduled" in their portal.
  • Massage is per person — each appointment carries its own guest, therapist, treatment and time.
  • Activities carry a guide (or pilot + vendor for fly-outs) and cap party size; the specific guide and boat are set on the scheduling calendar.

Maps to schema

Each request is an assignment (person + asset + trip + date) with a status; approval flips it to confirmed. Massage detail — therapist, treatment, time — lives in the assignment's details JSON, so the daily report and portal both read it. The SLA clock is tracked server-side.

07

Boats

Guests are seated on boats for their sportfishing — pick the day, then a group, then a boat. Each boat seats up to its set capacity, groups can share, and a guest keeps a "home boat" for the trip that can be switched for any single day. The key rule: on a day a guest has an adventure booked, that activity wins and they come off the boat.

Admin · dock

    Business rules

  • Seat guests by day → group → boat; each boat seats up to its set capacity (6, 4 or 2, from the crew setup).
  • Groups can share a boat, and if a group doesn't fully fit you choose who goes on it and place the rest elsewhere.
  • A guest has a home boat for the trip; a per-day switch overrides it for one day only.
  • Activity-priority rule: any day a guest has a scheduled adventure, that activity takes priority and they drop off the boat for the day.

Maps to schema

Boat seating is a per-person assignment of a Boat asset, keyed by trip and date (home seating with per-day overrides). Captain and deckhand come from the boat's asset_links. The activity-priority rule is resolved at read time against the day's confirmed adventures.

Phase D

On-property

While guests are at the lodge, operations run day to day: what everyone eats, what they catch and take home, and a single run sheet that puts every person in the right place each morning.

08

Breakfast & beverages

Each night, the lodge takes the next morning's breakfast and the day's beverage order — per guest. Breakfast is free choice from that day's menu; beverages roll onto the boat-stocking list by whichever boat the guest is on that day. It produces two outputs: a kitchen sheet and a boat-stocking list.

Admin · kitchen & dockGuest · can pre-set

    Business rules

  • Orders are captured per guest, per night, for the following morning.
  • Breakfast is free choice from that day's menu (mains + add-ons/mods).
  • Beverages are ordered by the 6-pack and roll onto the boat-stocking list for the boat that guest is on that day.
  • Two outputs come from the same order: a kitchen sheet (by guest) and a boat-stocking list (by boat).

Maps to schema

Each order is an assignment per guest per day, with the picks in details JSON. The menu and beverage catalogs are Breakfast and Beverage assets. Boat-stocking is derived by joining orders to the day's boat seating.

09

Fish processing

At the end of each fishing day, the group's catch is logged once. The engine yields filet weight per species (count × average weight × recovery %), totals the day, and splits it into however many take-home shares the group wants — one named guest per share. Each share's poundage lands on that guest's own record, so it rolls up per individual for the trip.

Admin · processing

    Business rules

  • Catch is logged per group, per fishing day, by species.
  • Filet yield = count × average weight × recovery %, summed to a day total in pounds of filet.
  • The day's filet is split into N take-home shares, each assigned to one named guest.
  • Each share's poundage attaches to that guest's record — the catch funnels to the individual, just like every other assignment.
  • Per guest: how the fish goes home (carry-on / ship), whether they keep collars & bellies, and who pays.

Maps to schema

fish_catch (trip, group, date, species, count, avg_weight, recovery) → fish_catch_split (share_index, person_id, lbs) → fish_payment (payer, method). Every share row references a person; no card number is ever stored, only a payment-method reference.

10

The daily run sheet

Everything above comes together here: the dock-and-spa run sheet for a single day, every assignment by individual guest. Boats list their captain, deckhand and seated guests; the spa lists each appointment by time; adventures list each guide's guests; and anyone not on the water is accounted for. This one sheet is why every stage insists on attaching to the person.

Admin · daily ops

    Business rules

  • The sheet is entirely by individual — boats name each seated guest under their crew; the spa names each guest by time; adventures name each guide's guests.
  • A guest booked on an adventure is pulled off the boat for that day (the activity-priority rule from Stage 7, made visible).
  • An evening massage keeps a guest on the water that day; a day-spa booking takes them off it.
  • Everyone present but not on a boat or activity is listed under "Enjoying the Island," grouped by party — nobody is unaccounted for.
  • The sheet pulls live from boat assignments and approvals, and prints for the dock.

Maps to schema

Pure read model: it joins the day's assignments (boats, activities, spa) with the trip roster from trip_profiles, applies the activity-priority rule, and groups the remainder. No new tables — it's the payoff of the individual-first design.

Phase E

Post-stay

A day after the guest checks out, the loop closes: a survey goes out, the answers become a score and a set of notes, and both attach back to the individual record — ready for the next visit.

11

The satisfaction survey

One day after checkout, each guest is automatically sent a pre-identified survey — short, on one standardized scale, grouped by department. If a rating is average or poor, it quietly asks how the lodge could do better. And if the guest is planning a return, it invites them to look at next year's calendar with a standing discount.

Guest · post-stay

    Business rules

  • Sent automatically one day after checkout, pre-identified to the guest (no "who are you?" step).
  • Ratings are standardized to one 4-point scale (Excellent / Good / Average / Poor), grouped by department.
  • Any Average or Poor rating reveals a "Tell us — how can we do better?" field, captured for service recovery.
  • Answering that they're planning a future stay reveals a rebook prompt to review next-year availability.
  • Business rule: a 10% discount is automatic for reservations made within 15 days of departure — and the survey lands inside that window.

Maps to schema

A survey response is another record type pointing at the guest's person (like an inquiry or a booking). Answers and write-ins are stored per question; the response feeds Stage 12's scoring.

12

Scoring & analysis

Every response becomes a number. Each rating maps to points, questions roll up into department scores, and the departments average into one guest satisfaction score from 0–100. Low answers flag the department for follow-up now; a monthly rollup goes to each department. Recommend and rebook are tracked separately as advocacy, not folded into the score.

Admin · reports

    Business rules

  • Each rating → points: Excellent 100 · Good 75 · Average 40 · Poor 0.
  • Questions roll up into departments — Service, Facilities, Culinary, Guiding, Spa — and the answered departments average into the 0–100 guest score.
  • Bands: 90–100 Exceptional · 75–89 Strong · 60–74 Watch · under 60 At-risk (auto-flags follow-up).
  • Any Average/Poor answer raises a service-recovery flag routed to that department.
  • Recommend and rebook are tracked separately as advocacy/retention, never inside the score.
  • Each department receives a monthly analysis of its own scores and comments.

Maps to schema

Scores are computed from the response and written to survey_scores, linked to the person and the trip. Department rollups and top-box percentages are derived; the latest and average score also cache onto the person for quick history.

13

It travels with the guest

The loop closes back to Stage 1. On the guest's staff-only profile, the score is published with a plain breakdown of how it was built — and, more importantly, their survey comments become carry-forward notes the lodge acts on in future years. A comment can catch what a score hides: a breakfast rated "excellent" whose note mentions a 30-minute wait becomes a flag for next season.

Admin · staff-only profile

    Business rules

  • The score is published on the guest's profile for staff eyes only — never shown to the guest.
  • The profile shows how the score was built (answer → points → department → score), so it's transparent to management.
  • Survey comments become carry-forward notes, tagged Fix or Delight, routed to a department and dated to the year.
  • Notes catch what the number hides — an "Excellent" breakfast whose comment flags a long wait still raises a Fix note.
  • Each note carries Carry-to-next-stay / Mark-addressed, and a year-over-year comment history shows the whole relationship.

Maps to schema

The score reads from survey_scores; the carry-forward notes are person-linked records with a tag, department, source year and status. Because they hang off the person, they resurface automatically the next time that guest books — the accumulation promised in Stage 1.
